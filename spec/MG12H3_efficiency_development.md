# MG128 — H3 exchangeable-token efficiency development

The next step removes combinatorial token-match enumeration while retaining the strict tick rule. This is a computational bridge toward an affordable stochastic particle study; it cannot itself establish a particle or a physical interpretation.

## Exact pruning argument

At a tick start, every identity match has an independent exponential mark. The first mark is an exponential race with total match rate. Once accepted, every incident match is permanently blocked. Removing these marks cannot alter any later accepted match. For surviving matches, conditioning on exceeding the last accepted time leaves independent exponential residual clocks by memorylessness. Thus one can race only surviving matches until the tick boundary. Products, arrivals and all reserved participants (including unchanged catalysts) are excluded from the available snapshot for the entire tick. The accumulated output is committed at the boundary. This is an equality in distribution of accepted events, not seed-by-seed equality or preservation of rejected-proposal traces.

Exchangeability permits grouping surviving matches by rule/site/destination and summing their rates. R4 uses choose(X_available,2)*Y_available*2/Omega^2 and reserves two X catalysts plus one Y; only Y changes to X. R3 reserves B and X. Unary and transport events reserve one token. Rates and fixed reference denominators remain frozen in form; available populations decrease, while output populations change separately. Parallel cell channels can be summed for count laws only; provenance/identity observables require a separate labelled lifting and are not claimed here. Pairwise-linear cell compilation is valid for current transport, not arbitrary future higher-order rules.

## Blocking checks before sampling

For all 896 saved reachable states of the four mixed fixtures, compare independent count channels against identity enumeration grouped jointly by output increment AND participant reservation at tolerance 1e-12 relative/absolute. This catches the otherwise invisible R4 catalyst mistake. Verify a strict-tick update subtracts the reservation only from availability, applies the increment only to output, conserves total count, and never introduces products to availability. Invalid counts/settings fail. Static checks contain no random draws or integrations.

Then build bounded timing and reference-law qualification with frozen seeds, uncertainty criteria, raw accepted-event retention and measured budget, committed before sampling. Exact-SSA count agreement alone is insufficient for tick qualification. Qualifications must include competition, overlap, catalyst reservation, boundary frames, no arrivals acting twice, and zero-rate/empty limits. Benchmark representative populations only after small-state checks; never infer full-lattice cost solely from tiny fixtures. Stop on discrepancy, preserve failures, no silent fixes to a registered outcome.

## Limits

This development uses the independently assembled count-transition formulas as a shared arithmetic component; tests compare to the identity engine. Formal reference comparisons must disclose that dependency and add a separate derivation. No throughput improvement, stochastic particle persistence, continuum limit or quantum behavior is claimed until tested. Reports use six significant digits unless a decision requires more; raw values retain precision.
