# MG11-G1 results — MG085

G1 supports a small but measurable dependence of resource supply on environmental connectivity: the bipartite network captured B resources more often than the prism network even though both had the same node count, degree, capacity, source-to-contact distance and per-token hop rate. This gives our explicit-reservoir programme a tested mechanism through which the structure of the environment can influence what reaches a reaction site. It reproduces a prediction of the chosen classical network model; it does not yet show that this mechanism sustains Model G particles, that the network emerges from a physical vacuum, or that A/B represent quantum entanglement.

## What remains open

This isolates a contrast between two selected fixed graph topologies. Equal degree, volume, distance and rate remove those simple explanations, but the experiment does not identify triangles alone as the cause or establish a universal geometry law. No Euclidean edge lengths or curvature are modeled. A later separately specified study would need to test whether topology-dependent supply affects the full nonlinear Model G pattern and its persistence, including depletion/feedback controls. Emergent environmental structure and a connection to entanglement remain additional modeling and evidential steps.
